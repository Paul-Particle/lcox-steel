# Test run, 13–14 September 2026

A full-matrix run over 2025: every route including the export twins, across
thirteen grid-connected areas and seven islanded ones, on assumptions moved onto
the research workbook's Model Input Register.

This note separates three things that are easy to blend together and should not
be: what was checked and how, what was decided and why, and what is being
asserted on nothing more than its having come out of the model. The third list is
the one that needs a human eye.

---

## What the run covers

| | areas |
|---|---|
| grid | DEU, ESP, FRA · NSW1, QLD1, SA1, TAS1, VIC1 · BR_SE, BR_S, BR_NE, BR_N · AB |
| islanded | DEU, ESP, FRA · AUS, BRA · AB, ONT |
| sensitivity | the seven islanded areas, MOE turndown 0.70 |

Routes: `h2-only`, `h2-dri-eaf`, `ng-dri-eaf`, `mix-dri-eaf`, `moe-eaf`, `ew-eaf`
and the five `-export` twins. Iron is exported to Spain; finished steel stays
where it was made. One gas price everywhere, no carbon price, one grid charge
expressed per MW with no volumetric component.

Three geographies are new and two of them are incomplete on purpose — showing
where the data runs out was part of the point.

## 1. Checked, and how

- **Brazilian submarket geometries** — cut from Natural Earth Admin 1, within 2%
  of the IBGE state areas they are made of (BR_SE −0.3%, BR_S −1.9%, BR_NE −0.3%,
  BR_N −0.4%). The four state lists together cover Brazil once and only once,
  which the IBGE totals confirm to 0.03%.
- **Unknown emission intensity reports as unknown** — verified on real Brazilian
  runs: LCOS present, `emissions_kg_co2e_per_t_steel` blank rather than zero,
  `el_eaf_gwh` still populated because the MWh drawn is known whatever the mix
  was, and `emissions_unavailable_reason` naming the area and the cause.
- **Alberta's capacity factors** — wind-onshore 0.304 and solar 0.178, over 1414
  eligible cells, matching the values the failed pipeline run had already logged.
- **All three dashboard pages build**, and the hub assembles at 10.3 MB against
  its 16 MB limit.
- **Test suite** passes (104 passed, 9 skipped on absent caches).

## 2. Decided, and why

- **Ontario runs islanded only.** IESO's yearly HOEP report ends 2025-04-30: the
  Market Renewal Program replaced HOEP with locational marginal prices on 1 May
  2025, and nothing here reads those. An earlier year is not a substitute, since
  HOEP excludes the Global Adjustment and would put a commodity-only price against
  all-in prices everywhere else. Eleven missing networks would have blocked the
  whole grid report, not left an Ontario-shaped hole in it.
- **Grid charge is one capacity fee, 140 k€/MW/yr, with the volumetric part at
  zero.** At this plant's ~8000 full-load hours that is near 19 €/MWh, between the
  German HV band-load position and Hydrogen Europe's 29.3 €/MWh EU average. One
  number so the geographies are compared on the same basis, given that much of a
  real network bill is missing for most of them anyway.
- **Hydrogen storage is a salt cavern at 350 €/MWh** — a deliberate lower bound.
  The register's tank-with-compressor figure is 60 050 €/MWh.
- **MOE and EW metallic yield are 1.03 and 1.04**, not the register's 1.08 for
  both. The register applies one generic ~93.5% EAF yield to every charge and lets
  the Fe assay do the rest, so its figure carries no information about either
  process. MOE taps carbon- and silicon-free iron and its cell is its own slag
  channel, so it does not pay the pig-iron burn-off that produces 1.08.
- **The furnace gained a fourth charge state.** The register prices briquettes and
  electrolytic iron apart and one `cold` state could not hold both.

## 3. Asserted on the model alone — needs a human eye

Both headline orderings turn on a single input sitting near one end of its own
stated range. Neither is a finding about the world.

**Electrowinning now beats MOE everywhere** — but the grid result rests entirely
on this run's capex change. Each €1/(t·yr) of EW capex is 0.098 €/t steel, so
reverting 708 → 1200 adds 48.3 €/t, more than every grid margin. The break-even
capex at which MOE retakes first place is **below 1200 in every grid case**
(852 ESP to 1110 SA1) — below the value the model used the day before. The file's
own comment says public estimates span 500–3000, so the grid ranking is decided
inside the bottom fifth of a range it already admits to. The islanded result is
robust by comparison, surviving to 1700–3700.

**Fossil beats green everywhere, by 81–185 €/t (11–24%)** on matched pairs. NG-DRI
burns 4.06 MWh of gas per tonne of steel, so the ordering reverses between
**45 and 67 €/MWh** gas depending on geography — Australia first, Spain last. The
run uses 21.9 €/MWh, the register's low/high midpoint, near the bottom of the
plausible band; TTF has run roughly 30–45 through 2025.

Separately, the percentage premium is *understated*: the all-in plant cost is a
large near-constant on every route, so it inflates both levels and compresses the
ratio. The same absolute premium on a conventional cost base reads as 18–35%.

**The export comparison is not about freight.** At the register's shipping factor,
Australia to Spain is about 6 €/t over 17 960 km, so what separates an export
route from its domestic twin is the cold charge and the destination's power price,
not the ocean.

## Known gaps

- No emission intensity for the four Brazilian submarkets or Alberta: ONS
  publishes an undivided thermal aggregate, AESO no generation at all.
- No Ontario grid case (above).
- Anchor-colocation was not run; the p95 best-site variant is an upper bound on
  resource quality.
- WACC is one global 7%. The register is per country and per asset class.
