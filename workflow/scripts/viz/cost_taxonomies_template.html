Green steel model · cost breakdown

Test run 2 · 2026-09-14

Every steel route including its export twin, across 13 grid-connected zones and 7 islanded geographies on the 2025 weather year, plus Alberta and Ontario on 2024 — 282 networks on assumptions moved onto the research workbook's Model Input Register. Renewables are sited best-site P95 (an upper bound on resource quality); hydrogen storage is a salt cavern at 350 €/MWh (a lower bound); the grid charge is a single 140 k€/MW/yr capacity fee with no volumetric part; one gas price everywhere. Iron is exported to Spain, finished steel stays where it was made. Use the Grid control to move between the grid-connected and islanded halves; the Sensitivity control carries the one commissioned variant, MOE turndown at 70%, now run on both halves — worth 55–131 €/t islanded and almost nothing on grid. Hover any segment for the inputs behind it.

Brazilian and Canadian grid data is not verified and incomplete. The four Brazilian submarkets and both Canadian provinces publish a market price but no generation mix, so those runs report emission intensity as unknown rather than as a number. Ontario runs on 2024, its last full year before the Market Renewal Program replaced HOEP on 2025-05-01, and HOEP is commodity-only: it leaves out the Global Adjustment, which is most of what an Ontario consumer pays. That series averages 20.6 €/MWh against Germany's 89.3, and the gap is the missing charge rather than cheap power — which is the point worth looking at, not the ranking.

LCOE — levelised cost of electricity · € / MWh delivered

Each route sizes its own electricity system, so LCOE is a per-route number. The two taxonomies differ only in that the alternative separates renewables capital from its fixed O&M — shown side by side anyway, so the pair can be read the same way throughout.

Each route sizes its own electricity system, so LCOE is a per-route number rather than one figure for the geography. Renewables carry their capital apart from their fixed O&M.

LCOE is route-wide: an export route's destination power band is the electricity its melt draws abroad, counted here because those megawatt-hours are in the denominator either way.

Only the routes that build an electrolyser have one. The alternative splits the electrolyser three ways — capital, fixed O&M and the water/variable opex it consumes.

Every route is on the axis. A route that builds an electrolyser carries it split three ways — capital, fixed O&M and the water/variable opex it consumes. The same LCOS as above, cut as finely as the model allows — each plant's annualised capital apart from its fixed O&M, the electrolyser's water apart from its capital, renewables by technology, the battery's power apart from its energy, the grid connection apart from the energy it carries.
