Reading this. LCOS is the full levelised cost per tonne of liquid steel: annualised capex plus operating cost of the whole system — renewables, storage, grid, process plant, ore and consumables — divided by annual output. The cost breakdown and built capacity panels are the project's exact pipeline figures (all route/siting scenarios). Clean means the zero-fossil routes (H2-DRI, MOE, EW); clean premium is the cheapest of those minus the fossil NG-DRI-EAF benchmark, and break-even CO₂ is the carbon price that closes it, at tCO₂/MWh of natural gas. LCOE is the electricity-system cost (renewables + battery + grid + transmission) per MWh generated; LCOH is the hydrogen cost (electrolyser + buffer + its electricity at LCOE) per MWh LHV.

Routes. H2-DRI-EAF hydrogen DRI + electric arc furnace · MOE molten-oxide electrolysis · EW aqueous electrowinning — these three are zero-fossil. MIX-DRI-EAF is a transitional route: two DRI shafts (H₂ and gas) feed one iron bus and the optimiser picks the blend — the H₂ share column shows how much iron it made from hydrogen. Where that share falls to ~zero (typically on grid power, where gas undercuts H₂) the route is flagged H₂ rejected and is economically identical to the fossil benchmark. NG-DRI-EAF is natural-gas DRI on grid power (the fossil benchmark); the natural-gas price assumed for these routes is shown above the table. Cards compare the best siting (country-average vs best-site P95) per route. Values are near-optimal within input uncertainty — read rankings and ranges, not the last digit.
